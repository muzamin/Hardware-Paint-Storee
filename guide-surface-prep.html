LABORATORY SEQUENCE

1. The 4-Stage Substrate Remediation Protocol

01

Decontaminate with Non-Residue TSP

Grease films and soap scum act as non-stick barriers. Wash surfaces using a damp sponge saturated with phosphate-free TSP substitute. Rinse thoroughly with clean warm water and permit 24 hours of forced-air drying before proceeding to mechanical abrasion.

02

Progressive Abrasive Sanding (80 → 150 → 220 Grit)

Begin with 80-grit on flaking edges to eliminate detached layers. Transition to 150-grit to scuff high-gloss finishes and create mechanical tooth. Finish with 220-grit across vinyl compound patches for an imperceptible flush blend. Wipe with a microfiber tack cloth.

03

Equalize Porosity with Dedicated Primer

Joint compound absorbs paint 300% faster than paper drywall face. Apply a full uniform coat of high-hide bonding primer. On water-stained ceilings or knotty pine timber, specify shellac-base primer to permanently block tannin bleed.

04

Cut-In & Roll with Wet-Edge Discipline

Cut in ceiling margins using a solid chiseled sash brush no more than 10 minutes ahead of the roller. Roll in contiguous 4-foot vertical bands, overlapping each stroke by 50% while the paint remains wet to avoid unsightly lap lines.

3. Frequently Asked Questions

Never paint water-based paint directly over oil paint without conversion. Water-based paint cannot adhere to the smooth, non-porous alkyd surface and will peel off in sheets. Test the surface with denatured alcohol on a rag: if paint softens, it is water-based; if it remains hard, it is oil. To convert, sand thoroughly with 150-grit, apply a dedicated acrylic conversion bonding primer, then finish with your chosen topcoat.
Exterior masonry and stucco must measure below 12% moisture on a calibrated non-destructive meter. Fresh poured concrete requires a mandatory 28-day curing cycle to allow high internal alkalinity (pH > 13) to neutralize before applying mineral silicate paint.
