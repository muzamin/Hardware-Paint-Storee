The Micro-Film Rolling Sequence

Achieving a velvety, streak-free surface requires methodical loading. Saturate 3/8″ microfiber sleeves evenly, roll ceiling-to-floor in overlapping ‘W’ passes, and never stretch thin paint over dry edges.

STEP 01

Surface Decontamination

Scrub walls with low-residue TSP substitute to eliminate airborne kitchen greases, candle soot, and fingerprints. Check substrate moisture with a pinless meter (under 12% moisture required).

Required Equipment:
Cellulose sponge, TSP degreaser, digital moisture detector.
STEP 02

Mechanical Abrasion & Patch

Feather-edge existing glossy coatings with 150-grit abrasive sheets. Fill screw dents and micro-hairlines with lightweight non-shrinking vinyl compound, followed by 220-grit dry sanding.

Required Equipment:
Aluminum oxide sandpaper (150/220), flexible joint knife, vacuum tack cloth.
STEP 03

Targeted Primer Bonding

Apply breathable mineral silicate primer over bare plaster or alkali masonry. For drywall patches, apply high-hide stain-blocking primer to equalize porosity and prevent flash spots.

Required Equipment:
FORGELUX High-Hide Primer, 1/2″ microfiber roller, 14-day tape.
STEP 04

Two-Coat Topcoat Master

Cut in ceiling borders and baseboards using a 2.5″ chiseled sash brush. Maintain a continuous wet edge using an architectural roller, rolling in ceiling-to-floor 'W' patterns without overworking.

Required Equipment:
2.5″ angled sash brush, 9″ heavy-duty roller cage, 9x12 drop canvas.
DEFECT AVOIDANCE MANUAL

3 Common Painting Mistakes & How to Prevent Them

Trade Standards: ASTM D3928
Mistake #1: Flashing / Lap Marks

Uneven Sheen Over Drywall Repairs

Applying topcoat paint directly over spackled patches causes the porous joint compound to suck moisture out of the paint, creating chalky dull spots when viewed at raking angles.

âœ” Trade Remedy: Always spot-prime compound patches with 1 dedicated coat of high-hide primer prior to rolling whole walls.
Mistake #2: Peeling Tape Bleed

Jagged Edges Along Baseboard Moldings

Leaving masking tape on walls until paint is bone-dry creates a rubbery acrylic film across the tape edge. Pulling it tears the freshly cured paint layer away from the substrate.

âœ” Trade Remedy: Peel masking tape while topcoat is still damp to the touch at a sharp 45-degree angle downward.
Mistake #3: Micro-Blistering

Trapped Humidity Under Enamel Films

Painting in humid weather (>75% RH) or over damp masonry traps water vapor beneath the drying paint film. As temperatures warm, moisture evaporates, forcing bubbles to the surface.

âœ” Trade Remedy: Paint strictly when indoor temperatures are 55°F–80°F and relative humidity is below 65%.
